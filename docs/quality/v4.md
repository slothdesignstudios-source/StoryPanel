## 2. Reveal timing

Continuity rules stay what they are: facts that hold in every panel they apply to. Story timing is
now separate. Each panel can give a **state** for up to four story elements, named the same way each
time:

| State | Meaning | What the picture is asked | What the panel check accepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| absent | not in the scene yet | "Not in this scene yet: …" | not visible |
| hidden | in the scene, hidden from the reader | "… is here but hidden from the reader: keep it covered or out of frame" | not visible |
| unnoticed | visible to the reader, unnoticed by the characters (deliberate foreshadowing) | "… small and secondary, not the focal point; no character looks at it" | visible |
| revealed | revealed to both | "Show clearly: …" | visible |

- **Script.** The writer gives the states and keeps each panel's action consistent with them. They
  are editable per panel ("Story elements and their timing").
- **One-time events and rules.** The Story Bible prompt says one-time events and reveals are never
  continuity rules.
- **Conflicts.** A must rule that contradicts the approved timing becomes a **review item**: "A
  continuity rule conflicts with the story's timing" (Train rule 4 in panels 1–3). The rule is never
  rewritten; you except it there or change the timing.
- **Scripts without states.** These fall back to the earlier word heuristic, as a warning.
- **Exceptions keep their meaning.** "Does not hold here; follow the action above". An exception
  never turns into "absent".
- **References.**
  - A location reference whose description names an element that's absent or hidden in this panel
    is **left out**, and the reason is recorded.
  - A previous panel that showed the element is left out the same way.
  - Otherwise, a panel whose state differs from the reference gets the reference limited to
    architecture and light.
  - **This is wording on top of a word heuristic: it can't guarantee what an image model draws.** The
    panel check is the safeguard.
- **Panel check.** Same call, no extra cost. It reports whether each element is visible.
  - A clear answer against the state is a defect, repaired once with a concrete note; the existing
    retry cap and repeat-stop apply.
  - "Uncertain" goes to review, with no paid repair.
- **Story Bible check.** It judges early reveals as advice, and treats deliberate hints as fine.

## 3. Lettering, verified on the existing pages (no art regenerated)

`reletter.py` takes the art layer out of each exported PDF and re-letters it with the current code.
It does this twice, with **guessed** faces (as happened) and with **located** faces (read off the art
by eye, standing in for the check's boxes). It renders PNG with the app's PNG renderer and PDF with
the app's PDF exporter.

| Page | Guessed faces (as exported) | Located faces |
|---|---|---|
| Train p4 | still over Teo's face; now flagged "possibly covers a face" and "tail may miss its speaker" | 0% face overlap; balloon in the free window area |
| Train p2 | over Teo's hair; flagged | both balloons clear of faces, read top to bottom |
| Train p6 | flagged | tail ends at Mira |
| Train p3 | flagged | thought cloud can't avoid Mira's close-up head: **"covers a face" (confirmed)** with editing choices |
| Tulips p3 | flagged | balloon moves off the cap, below Walter's face |

PNG and PDF of the same re-lettered page match in layout. The mean pixel difference is 7–10 of 255,
from resampling.

What changed:
- **Located vs guessed faces.**
  - Faces the check located are hard limits.
  - A guessed face can't be avoided reliably: steering by a wide guess made Train p2 *worse* (97% over
    Teo's face), so guesses don't steer.
  - A guessed face is reported as **"possibly: may cover a face"** wherever a balloon falls in that
    character's area, so it is never passed as clear.
- **Required faces from the check.** The v4 panel check is required to give a face and body box for
  every visible character, and records `faces_unlocated` when it doesn't.
- **Artwork detail.** The letterer prefers plain areas of the actual artwork (sky, walls), measured
  locally. This alone didn't move Train p4's balloon off Teo's face; real face positions did.
- **Reading order.** It works in either arrangement of speakers. A balloon left of the previous one
  must sit clearly lower, or it is flagged "Reads out of order". Nobody is moved to the left.
- **Tails.** A tail ending nearer another character than its speaker is flagged. A tail aimed at a
  guessed face is flagged as uncertain.
- **Sound effects.** They avoid faces and characters as strongly as balloons do, and are flagged when
  they cover a character.
- **No gutter balloons.** Balloons stay inside the panel. When there's no safe place, the editor and
  the export say so, with the choices: move, shorten, or redraw to leave room.
- **Exports.** Exports list unresolved lettering problems per page and panel:
  - **confirmed:** face, object, overflow, order, tail;
  - **uncertain:** "possibly…", when the face positions were guessed.

## 4. Cast review

- **Propagation.** The sheet notice now says that a defect approved on the sheet usually repeats in
  later panels.
- **Revision.** The fix button reads "Put this fix in the revision box"; credit terms are unchanged
  (1 credit, only if a new sheet is delivered).
- **Layout checks, fixtures first.**
  - A full-body view running off the image edge used to pass the layout check. It is now measured
    ("touches the image edge, probably cut off (measured)").
  - Close-ups that fill their cells but are separated by clean gutters are now a valid grid.
  - A sheet that really isn't a grid stays unverified and is still reported.
  - No threshold was loosened.
- **Messages name the check.** "The review saw…" (model judgment), "(measured)" (image analysis),
  "couldn't be separated automatically, so they weren't checked" (not checked).
- **Small features.** The sheet check also sees the expression close-ups, for small facial features.

## Remaining uncertainties

- **Why faces weren't located** in these comics: the dossier's `head_boxes` and `faces_unlocated` will
  say. Until then, the fix makes the problem visible rather than solving it.
- **Whether the image model honours** element states, limited references and lettering space. Wording
  can't guarantee it; the live comparison measures it.
- **Heuristic conflict detection.** Rule–timing and reference conflicts are found by matching words,
  and can miss differently worded rules.
- **Model judgments.** The early-reveal and element-visibility judgments are model judgments; their
  accuracy isn't measured here.
